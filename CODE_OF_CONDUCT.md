# ClassiGuard CyFence: Code of Conduct & Ethical Security Research Policy

## Our Pledge

In the interest of fostering an open, welcoming, and responsible cybersecurity research community, we as contributors, maintainers, and operators of **ClassiGuard CyFence** pledge to conduct all software evaluations, vulnerability audits, and interactions in a manner that respects human rights, laws, privacy, and systemic integrity.

---

## 1. Ethical Security Research & Responsible Conduct

As a user or evaluator of this tool, you commit to the following ethical pillars:

* **Strict Authorization First:** Never execute audits, scans, or probes against any system, network, domain, or IP address without prior documented authorization from the verified system owner.
* **Non-Destructive Testing:** ClassiGuard CyFence is strictly a defensive posture assessment tool. Users must never alter payloads or attempt denial-of-service, destructive flooding, data exfiltration, or service degradation.
* **Respect for User Privacy:** If an audit reveals exposed sensitive data (such as environment files or credentials), cease further interaction immediately, safeguard the finding securely, and notify the system owner directly through private, encrypted channels.
* **Responsible Disclosure:** Adhere to coordinated vulnerability disclosure best practices (including RFC 9116 `security.txt` protocols) and allow system owners a reasonable time window to remediate before public discussion.
* **No Weaponization:** Do not integrate this tool into automated attack frameworks, malware distribution channels, or non-consensual surveillance kits.

---

## 2. Community Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:
* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback on defensive security rules and configurations.
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall security community.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:
* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
* Public or private harassment.
* Publishing others' private information without explicit permission (doxxing).
* Promoting, demonstrating, or encouraging unauthorized attacks or cybercrime.

---

## 3. Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

---

## 4. Reporting Guidelines

Instances of abusive, harassing, or unethical behavior may be reported to the ClassiGuard Security Team at:
`security@classiguard.com` (or via private GitHub repository advisories).

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.
